## 1. Required outcome and scope

Every authoritative business decision must be evaluated by the policy engine against an approved, released installation policy. OPA is the common executable policy runtime. The same released rules govern automated outcomes, decisions to collect evidence or seek human review, and validation of a human response before its effects are applied.

An installation can change jurisdictions, institutional appetite, thresholds, product requirements, eligible automation, review authorities, evidence standards and escalation behavior, then test and release those changes. Editing a policy must not require changing a Python skill, TypeScript transform, workflow generator, UI component or database command implementation merely to remove a reference-domain business default.

This is broader than replacing the six checkpoint transforms in the two active agentic workflows. It includes direct case APIs, integration-triggered commands, canonical skill dispatch, hosted skills, source acquisition/use, human actions and external effects. A workflow diagram alone cannot establish enforcement.

### Decision versus computation

| Category | Examples | Target responsibility |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Observation or calculation | OCR, exact source spans, hashes, decimal arithmetic, ownership paths, name similarity, transaction totals, graph metrics, factual differences | Qualified, versioned skills/providers; emit attributed observations and limitations. No OPA call required for each arithmetic operation. |
| Business interpretation | Identity accepted, beneficial owner in scope, screening candidate cleared, risk tier adopted, EDD required, documents sufficient, reportable threshold met, review cadence selected | Released OPA policy gate; may consume versioned calculation results. |
| Consequential business action | Accept/decline, transition business status, clear an alert, accept a claim as fact, release a product, submit a filing, schedule a required review, delete eligible evidence | Policy-authorized command/effect, enforced by the owning service and bound to a decision receipt. |
| Human contribution | Correct an extracted field, resolve conflicting evidence, approve a policy-required exception | Authenticated observation/choice; a subsequent gate validates authority, scope, evidence and permitted effect. |
| Technical invariant | Tenant identity, signed execution scope, non-fabricated provenance, schema validity, idempotency, leases, immutable history, legal-hold existence | Service/database enforcement remains. A business pack cannot disable isolation or manufacture verified evidence. |

Labels such as `verified`, `valid`, `PASS`, `eligible`, `NEEDS_REVIEW`, `final: false` and `requiresHumanReview` need explicit semantics. A skill may report a source-stated status or recommend an interpretation; that value becomes business authority only through a gate. A skill's execution error remains a technical fact; the business response to missing evidence is policy-controlled.

Incomplete data can lead to further collection, a bounded retry, suspension, or human review. It must not automatically become rejection, clearance or a permanent human-only path. Complete data can still require a human if the released installation policy explicitly requires one.

## 4. Findings and remediation priorities

P0 means required before claiming a single policy authority or enabling automated business effects. P1 means required before broad domain adoption. P2 means operational/completeness work required before declaring the migration finished. Findings describe architectural gaps unless explicitly marked as an observed rejection; they are not claims of a proven exploit.

| ID | Priority | Finding, evidence and concrete remediation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| F01 | P0 | **Business restrictions are embedded in the policy engine's schemas.** `automaticDeclineEnabled` is literally false; finalization authority is literally HUMAN_ONLY; row/result dispositions and finalize constraints only admit APPROVED. E01. Introduce versioned, domain-neutral decisions/actions and policy-configured automation authority. Preserve legacy v1 parsing/replay; do not just change the YAML. |
| F02 | P0 | **The legacy OPA adapter is KYC-shaped and overrides auto-approval.** `policyAggregate` injects `automaticApprovalEnabled: false`; agentic packs now need a different per-case projection/authority. | F03 | P0 | **Human-review defaults exist at several layers.** Archived KYC forced REVIEW, the new pack field registry uses `falseFixed`, `evaluatePackGate(disposition)` always returns human review, and roster decision support declares `requiresHumanReview: always`/`final: false`. E14, E26, E28. Keep model outputs explicitly advisory; replace authoritative fixed review with installation policy and typed routing. Do not weaken archived behavior. |

## 5. Target architecture and contracts

The policy team supplies the interfaces below to the onboarding team; the onboarding team does not implement a second decision engine while waiting. Preserve section 5.2's typed decisions, section 5.4's owning-service application, and section 5.5's post-human evaluation as the integration contract referenced by the companion plan.

### 5.1 Installation policy lifecycle

Use the existing artifact/release registry and separation of duties. Proposed lifecycle:

`template → installation draft → compiled candidate → tested → independently approved → published → release-pinned → active`

A template's candidate status and unresolved owner inputs are editable installation inputs. Supply values with provenance or explicitly record a supported not-applicable/excluded-scope decision, compile/test, then release. Required inputs still unresolved for the enabled scope block activation; irrelevant modules should not block unrelated installed capabilities. This replaces a blanket inherited US-template block, not the approval/release boundary.

Start from the existing versioned baseline/jurisdiction/institution/product modules, `selection.yaml`, `composeEffectivePack`, `selectPolicyPack` and their fixtures. Extend this model for purpose and applicable regimes using a reviewed combination policy; do not invent an unrelated pack registry. Keep current tighten-only constraints as declared compatibility semantics for v1 packs. In v2, distinguish protected lower-layer constraints from editable institution defaults: automation options must not be permanently forced false by the engine, while an institution cannot silently relax a constraint it lacks authority to override. Test and release changes to the combination strategy itself.

Identify the institution, booking entity and applicable regimes from authorized facts. Preserve BankingRelationship's distinction between policy jurisdiction and booking geography, and require every requested product to be covered. Explain every included/excluded module. Missing applicability or an unresolved material conflict cannot become ALLOW. Human approval cannot silently waive a rule that the released pack does not permit overriding.

Resolve to an immutable effective-policy-set digest containing exact module versions/digests, input-profile versions, merge strategy, installation configuration digest and release identity. No mutable `latest` at evaluation. New policy releases change future authority according to an explicit effective-date/migration rule; they do not rewrite old receipts or silently repin existing cases.

Migrate each existing effective pack's ID/digest/layer provenance to this contract with explicit compatibility metadata. A composition digest (`pack.digest`), a compiled OPA bundle digest, and a released effective-policy-set digest identify different artifacts. Populate and verify per-gate `gateBundles`; do not rename one digest into another to satisfy legacy `policyAuthority` checks. A source file's `APPROVED` status is insufficient without the tenant registry/release authority.

### 5.2 Generic policy request and decision

Introduce an additive versioned decision contract rather than changing the meaning of existing `PolicyEvaluation.v1` or closed skill outputs in place. Proposed fields:

| Request envelope | Decision envelope |
| --- | --- |
| Tenant, installation, case/resource, subject/product scope, purpose and applicable regimes | Decision ID/digest, evaluator identity/version, effective policy set and module digests |
| Release/plan, gate/decision kind, input profile and schema version | Matched rules, reason codes, obligations, conflicts/gaps and evaluated facts/input digest |
| Snapshot ID/digest, case sequence, business dependency digest, as-of and knowledge cutoff | Decision state, scoped business actions, permitted automation or human requirements |
| Authorized evidence/observation versions, calculations and limitations | Exact authorized action payload fingerprints, resource scope, expiry, and action-consumption semantics |
| Proposed action or authenticated human response when applicable | Required reviewer authority/quorum when applicable; human decision references for a post-review decision |

Keep decision evaluation status distinct from business effect. Proposed states: `DECIDED`, `NEEDS_EVIDENCE`, `NEEDS_HUMAN`, `NOT_APPLICABLE`, and `UNRESOLVED`; technical failures remain errors. Proposed actions include approve/decline, adopt tier, clear/retain alert, collect evidence, request review, transition, schedule, submit, retain/delete, and no-effect outcomes. Final names require the contract PR, with explicit v1 adapters.

`BLOCK` must not mean customer decline. `ALLOW` to proceed with a workflow step must not authorize a different business action. Evidence collection is an explicit bounded action with source authority, not an unrestricted permission. A human choice is evidence to the post-review gate, not an authorization token for arbitrary commands.

R01 must expose the contract early for onboarding wave 2 topology tests with a stub evaluator. Stub decisions cannot mint applicable production grants. For the first pilot pack, configure the recorded human journey explicitly; separately test a released pack permitting automated outcomes. The pilot's human acceptance scenario must not reintroduce a universal human-only platform restriction.

Consume the onboarding/context work's subject/purpose and per-record provenance, admitted relationship projection and freshness contracts. New local Application/relationship/connector code is in progress in the shared workspace: verify its released contract and end-to-end hydration before closing C1–C5. Policy owns versioned authenticity `enabled`/`required` settings, per-party document requirements, risk weights/tier boundaries/model identity, EDD triggers, requirements/rules, permitted providers, automation and reviewer/quorum settings. ### 5.4 Enforcement at the write/effect boundary

Add a common protected-business-action registry in contracts and enforcement at each owning service. For each action specify its input profile, gate kind, allowed command/effect mapping, exact subject/resource/purpose and required evidence basis. Include fact/assessment adoption, direct case commands, schedules, external actions and integration-triggered mutations, not only final acceptance.

Resolve onboarding wave 2's work-item classification in R01: a command that creates assignable/executable work, a due schedule, a human queue entry or business routing is a protected action, regardless of the `ProposeWorkItem` name. Until an explicit inert proposal contract exists, treat the existing command as protected for agent-output expansion. An unresolved question, request or QA finding can be retained as a non-authoritative proposal/evidence item; a gate decides whether it creates work. Existing legacy behavior remains versioned during migration.

Likewise, `SubmitDecisionRecommendation` is a proposal, not disposition; a Finding proposal does not certify its truth; and proposed Control observations do not satisfy acceptance obligations until adopted through a gate. Coordinate the proposed `decision.requirements.compile` proposal-mode change with library owners and the onboarding team's producer work. Preserve `SKILL_FORBIDDEN_COMMANDS` and subject/schema-valid, non-adopted writes rather than broadly banning observations.

The platform executor loads a stored decision or verifies an authenticated scoped grant. A client cannot make a self-hashed payload authoritative. Check the current approved release, exact policy set, intended action and payload digest, tenant/case/subject scope, valid time, business dependencies, required human responses, revocation and idempotency.

Evaluate on snapshot S, then recheck its authority/dependencies under the transaction lock before persisting the decision, its authorized mutations and the audit/outbox records. Define receipt-consumption semantics so the evaluation's own ledger append does not invalidate its grant, while an intervening evidence or business-state change does. Do not implement an unexplained “S+1 is fresh” exception. Apply multi-action grants atomically where possible; otherwise persist per-action consumption and reconcile explicitly.

External effects require a durable outbox, action-scoped idempotency, current source/destination authority, expiry/revocation checks before send, and reconciliation of ambiguous delivery. A decision authorizing a filing does not imply it was filed. A transport retry cannot rerun a business effect with a changed payload. Revalidation of unchanged authority does not silently alter the business request.

Preserve `SKILL_FORBIDDEN_COMMANDS`: skills remain unable to create their own accepted fact, review, gate receipt or disposition. Replace the assumption “an external action always needs a human” with “an external action always needs a released decision grant; that grant may require a human.” Apply consequential actions through a qualified platform executor, not by relaxing the proposal API.

### 5.5 Human work and operational failure

Create human work from a gate's `NEEDS_HUMAN` result, carrying the decision/input references, allowed choices, required authority, quorum, independence constraints, expiry and gaps to resolve. Preserve reviewer identity and exact evidence viewed. Collect votes and conflict facts durably; let a post-review gate decide whether a permitted action can now execute.

Human corrections, newly acquired evidence, changed risk facts or a different policy scope can require a new input snapshot/evaluation. Revoked reviewers, stale approval, a changed action or an unresolved quorum conflict cannot authorize an effect. Technical assignment/lease/conflict handling stays in the workflow service. A service outage is an operational stop or retry, not a fabricated human-policy decision or a business rejection.

## 10. Rollout, observability and completion criteria

1. Ship contracts, validators and shadow recording with new behavior disabled. Keep legacy active releases executing their existing semantics; block any claim that the old path provides universal gate coverage.
2. Compile representative installed packs and run differential shadow evaluation without effects. Classify differences as intended policy changes, projection defects, engine parity defects or legacy contradictions. Review every material difference rather than accepting an aggregate accuracy number.
3. Enable v2 enforcement for one explicit tenant/jurisdiction/product/purpose and a narrow action set. Require qualified skills/providers and a released policy. Exercise the automatic path, evidence collection path and human path through actual command/outbox ownership boundaries.
4. Expand by action family and domain only after sink coverage and qualification evidence exist. Prevent a new integration, skill, command or business-output adoption from introducing an unclassified action. Use contract registration and sink tests as the guard; simple text searches are review aids, not proof of enforcement.
5. Roll back by disabling new work for the affected policy/action scope and activating an approved prior release with defined case migration. Do not erase completed decisions, reverse external effects implicitly, or change a receipt's policy digest. Reconcile held/in-flight work explicitly.

Expose metrics and inspectable receipts for evaluations by gate/policy/action, needs-evidence/human rates, technical failures, stale grants, denied bypass attempts, replay/duplicate effects, pending/revoked approvals, source-use denials and added latency. Do not log raw sensitive evidence. Publish per-domain/action coverage with named denominators; an OPA node count or a green provider probe is not decision coverage.

Completion requires all of the following:

- Every authoritative business sink and adopted business-output field has an owner, gate/input profile, protected action and test evidence; the 229-skill inventory has no unresolved authoritative consumers.
- The two active agentic domains use typed gate results for business routing/effects; the screening slice is integrated or retained as an explicitly scoped transition fixture. Archived flows preserve their legacy semantics/digests. No new raw skill predicate, pack/banking transform, task failure policy or UI role string independently authorizes a business outcome.
- Two installation packs demonstrably change automation, appetite, evidence and review behavior without application-code edits; incomplete/conflicted data and missing jurisdiction coverage behave as their reviewed policies specify.
- Automated and post-human outcomes both pass current receipt/action enforcement. Skills/models remain proposal-only for consequential authority.
- Real OPA conformance is mandatory and non-skipped; contract, database, HTTP integration, cross-runtime, browser, migration and image checks pass with inspectable artifacts.
- New versions can be released and old versions replayed. Revocation, rollback, cancellation, outbox reconciliation and qualified production scope have been demonstrated.

For the first pilot, apply these criteria to the explicitly qualified core/action scope and the human-required pilot pack. Broader 229-skill authoritative-consumer coverage is the consolidation completion bar, not a prerequisite to prove the first narrow journey. Unqualified optional skills, unsupported trust semantics and unresolved required policy fields remain unavailable or blocked, never silently passed.

Remaining input work is explicit: institution-specific rule content, applicability/overrides, supported automation scope, reviewer authorities and real qualification evidence are supplied during installation/release. They are not reasons to keep unchangeable business restrictions in platform code, and they are not evidence that every candidate reference capability is production-ready.
