# Process composition: scope, due diligence, triggers and the plan catalogue

The agentic KYC/KYB processes are composed, not hand-written. A process is defined by its **scope**,
and `@ehf/flow-composer` (`packages/flow-composer`) derives everything else from the agent roster
(`domains/agents`), the policy pack layers (`domains/policy`) and the skill library.

## Scope

| Dimension | Values | What it decides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customer type | `individual`, `corporate`, `trust` | Which roster agents serve it, the skills that cannot apply (company registry, ownership sanctions for a person), the identity acquisition (registry, LEI, officers, ownership for a company) and the document requirements package. A trust is a legal arrangement: the business-banking packs and entity agents cover it, with the library's `trust` documents, no company registry, officers or ownership discovery (its parties come from the trust deed), and never simplified due diligence. The packs screen a trust's trustees, settlor, protector and beneficiaries, require a named trustee (`required_party_roles.trust`), and ask no shareholding of it |
| Trigger | `onboarding`, `periodic_review`, `event_review` (+ `event`) | Onboarding and periodic review run every agent; an event review runs the review core (profile, screening, adverse media, risk, decision, QA) plus the agents its event can affect (`REVIEW_EVENTS`), and resolves that event's documents |
| Due diligence | `simplified` (SDD), `standard` (CDD), `enhanced` (EDD) | See below |
| Jurisdictions | ISO alpha-2 with a pack layer (US, GB, AU) | One effective pack per jurisdiction; a jurisdiction without a layer is refused with the remedy |
| Products | card, deposit, payments, lending, merchant_acquiring | The pack is composed for exactly these products, so a deposit-only business does not inherit merchant acquiring's adverse-media requirement |

`resolveFlow(scope)` returns the flow definition and a **decision for every agent, skill, connector,
pack and requirement**, with its basis (a scope attribute, a pack field and the layer that set it,
or a roster attribute). `composeForScope` produces the package, workflow, business policy and scope
file; `previewScopeChange` reports what a scope change adds and removes.

## Due-diligence levels (`DUE_DILIGENCE_RULES`)

| | SDD | CDD | EDD |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Applies to | An eligible legal entity: listed company, government body, regulated institution | Retail customers, ordinary businesses | PEPs, high-risk countries, shell companies, a high risk tier |
| Identity | Entity status (registry) | Identity documents (CIP) | Independent corroboration |
| Beneficial ownership | Exempt (no ownership agent or acquisition) | Owners over 25 percent | Mapped through every layer (control, nominee, circular ownership as required steps) |
| Source of funds | Not required | Declared purpose | Verified with documents (source of wealth too, for a person); decision-ready requires it |
| Review | Every three years | Every year | Every six months; never the low-risk carve-out |

## Plan catalogue and case pinning

Released processes record their scope on their checkpoints (`processScope`). A case names its
scope and `POST /v1/case-plans/select` (control-api, `case-plans.ts`, `selectCasePlan`) returns the
one released plan for its customer type, trigger, event and due-diligence level, and that plan's
one pinned pack for its jurisdiction whose product set holds every product. The case is pinned to
the **pack** (id and digest): the checkpoints choose their policy set and every policy-reading skill
its projection by that pin. No plan, no pack, or more than one is refused with the reason.

The repository ships five starter processes (generated by `scripts/sync-agentic-flows.ts`, seeded
as deployment drafts): KYC and KYB standard (US, GB), KYC-EDD and KYB-EDD, and KYB-SDD (US, GB, AU).
Any other scope is composed from the canvas (scope editor on a policy gate) and released like any
draft.

The create-case sheet (web, `create-case.tsx`) asks for the scope: customer type, trigger (and the
review event), due diligence (levels the customer type cannot take are disabled), policy jurisdiction
and products. It shows the selected process and pack, or the refusal and what to do, before the case
is created; the BFF re-makes the selection when it creates the case and pins it to that plan, pack
authority and the pack's jurisdiction.

## EDD escalation

When the EDD triggers fire (`edd.trigger.evaluate`, which sees the tier and the sanctions, PEP and
adverse-media outcomes), the step records an `edd_required` claim. A standard or simplified
process's decision-ready checkpoint then answers NEEDS_EVIDENCE (`EDD_ESCALATION_REQUIRED`) with an
`EDD_ESCALATION` work item instead of deciding the case. When that work item's event reaches the
control plane, it moves the case onto the released enhanced process for the same customer type,
trigger and pinned pack (jurisdiction and products) and starts its run (`case-escalation.ts`). A case
worker can also run it (`POST /v1/cases/:caseId/due-diligence/escalate`, idempotent).

The move is a `ReplanCase` command the control plane issues in its own name: `case.replan` is held
only by the `Case.Replan` workload role, never by a person, an agent or an integration. The case store
accepts it only while the EDD_ESCALATION work item is open, completes that item in the same
transaction, and records `case.replanned` with both pins. Where a run needs an admitted source
context first (sourced plans, production), the operator starts it.
